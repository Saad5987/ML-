01 · Abstract

01 / 09

What was investigated?

This study asks which observable search-performance signals can help identify content pages that deserve human review. The analysis uses aggregated search and engagement metrics from the FlyRank ML Internship dataset and evaluates a Decision Tree using both random and time-aware validation.

Observed accuracy was 1.0000 on the random split and 0.9999 on the time-aware check, but a target-feature dependency was identified as a leakage concern. The final output is therefore treated as directional decision-support rather than evidence of causal impact or production-level predictive performance.

01
Research position

High observed accuracy is reported transparently, but leakage means it should not be treated as proof of predictive power.

02 · Introduction

02 / 09

Problem statement

Content teams often need to decide which pages deserve investigation first. A large search dataset can contain millions of observations, making manual prioritization difficult.

This work focuses on a narrower decision: identifying pages whose observed search performance suggests that human review may be worthwhile.

DECISION SUPPORTED
Prioritize pages for investigation using observable search-performance signals.

The final content decision remains with human reviewers.

06 · Limitations

06 / 09

What this work cannot claim

Leakage

The target used impressions and clicks while those variables were also included as features.

Limited validation

The time-aware check used the earlier 50,000-row sample and does not establish broad future performance.

Observational data

Associations do not establish that changing content will cause better rankings, clicks, traffic, or engagement.

Human judgment remains necessary

Search intent, content quality, technical issues, seasonality, and business context are not fully captured.
